Understanding Heuristics

An Heuristic is a "rule of thumb" that estimates how far a state is from the goal. A* uses it to prioritize which paths to explore.

Misplaced Stickers

Analogy: "Peel and Stick"

Imagine you peel off every sticker that is in the wrong place and stick it back where it belongs. We just count how many stickers we moved.

  • Pros: Very fast to calculate.
  • Cons: Dumb. It thinks a piece 1 move away is the same as one 3 moves away.

Manhattan Distance (3D)

Analogy: "The Taxi Driver"

Imagine each piece is a taxi that needs to drive through the city (grid) to get home. We add up all their distances.

Example:
A piece is at coordinate (0, 0, 0) but belongs at (2, 2, 2).
Distance = |2-0| + |2-0| + |2-0| = 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 moves away.
We do this for all 26 pieces and sum it up.
  • Pros: Smart. It knows exactly how "far" the cube is from being solved.
  • Cons: Takes a bit more math to calculate.

Why it matters: A better heuristic (like Manhattan) guides the search more directly to the goal, exploring fewer useless states (nodes).
